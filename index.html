APFEA: AI Agents Across the Whole Foundry Process Flow

APFEA — the AI-assisted Phenomenological Foundry Engineering Approach — puts AI agents on top of the entire path a chip travels, from design and GDS through wafer fabrication, packaging and final test. The agents record what actually happens at every stage, feed it back into the design, and flag shrinking design margin before tape-out. The goal is stated in one line: eliminate the mask-fix re-tape-out.

The core idea in sixty seconds

A design house tapes out a chip designed against the foundry's PDK — the process design kit, with its nominal transistor thresholds. But real silicon never quite matches the PDK. The gap between the two is where margin quietly disappears, and it is usually discovered the expensive way: after fabrication, in a failed or marginal chip, followed by a mask fix and a re-tape-out that costs months and a full new mask set.

APFEA's answer is to stop treating that gap as a surprise. Put a process guru — a silicon technologist — beside the chip architect from day one, and give both of them AI agents that watch the whole flow: every stage records its data into a shared thread, the recorded phenomenology (how this process actually behaves, on this kind of chip) is analyzed continuously, and the findings flow back into the design while there is still time to act on them.

The gap nobody owns

Design houses tend to assume the foundry fab will resolve all process issues. But foundry engineers don't know the design's details — and one foundry technology manager may be covering a dozen tape-out projects at once. The design team, for its part, sees the process only through the PDK's nominal numbers. The interface between the two is where re-tape-outs are born.

The goalkeeper

“A good goalkeeper can prevent the team from being downgraded to the next tier.”

APFEA's organizational claim: a design house needs its own process expertise in the room from the beginning through product qualification — an early-warning role, not a rescue role. The AI agents exist to give that goalkeeper eyes on every stage of the flow.

The process flow, stage by stage

The Foundry Process Flow AGT spans the full journey of a chip — nine stages, end to end. At each stage the system fixes four things in writing: what comes in, what goes out, what gets recorded, and the gate a lot must pass — plus the feedback that flows back upstream. Pick a stage:

Stage 1 — Chip design & GDS

Where APFEA starts: not at the fab, but at the drawing board. The design is built against an anticipated process — Vt values pre-estimated from PDK test-chip versus customer-chip differences and from history — and every functional block carries a recorded margin budget. The agent's job here is to know, block by block, how much margin each circuit actually has.

InputPDK, design specification, historical silicon data
OutputGDS layout with per-block margin records
RecordedAnticipated-Vt assumptions, corner-simulation results, margin per block
GateNo block proceeds with margin below its agreed floor

Feedback: margin findings from every later stage return here first — this is the stage the whole loop exists to serve.

Stage 2 — Tape-out

The decision point APFEA is built to protect. Before the mask order goes out, the pre-tape-out check re-runs the design — functional blocks and the full chip — under corner-lot-split DOE conditions (device parameters swept to their ±3σ corners, mirroring real silicon corner lots). A block whose margin collapses at a corner is redesigned now, while redesign is still cheap.

InputFinal GDS, margin records from Stage 1
OutputMask order — or a targeted redesign list
RecordedPre-tape-out margin report, corner-lot-split simulation results
GateFull-chip margin sign-off under corner conditions

Feedback: a failed gate loops back to Stage 1 — by design, not by disaster.

Stage 3 — Wafer fabrication

Silicon starts moving, and so does the data. Wafer-lot and chamber-level records are captured as the lot is processed, and each die begins accumulating its unique digital thread — the continuous record that will follow it to the system level. Measured device behavior is compared against the anticipated values the design was built on.

InputMask set, process recipe, lot plan
OutputProcessed wafers with per-lot, per-chamber records
RecordedWafer-lot / chamber AGT data, in-line measurements, actual vs. anticipated device parameters
GateLot proceeds within recorded process windows

Feedback: actual-vs-anticipated deviations update the Vt model used by the next design — the loop learns from every lot.

Stage 4 — Interposer layout & fabrication

For advanced packages, the interposer is a process flow of its own — layout, fabrication, and its own yield behavior. APFEA treats it as a first-class stage rather than an afterthought, because in a 3D stack the interposer's phenomenology (including TSV stress) becomes part of the chip's electrical environment.

InputPackage plan, die maps, interposer design
OutputFabricated interposers with yield records
RecordedInterposer yield data, TSV stress-zone maps
GateKnown-good interposers only, into stacking

Feedback: stress-zone and yield findings return to package and chip design.

Stage 5 — Wafer sort

The first full electrical verdict on the silicon. Sort data is where anticipated-vs-actual stops being a model and becomes a measurement: which dies pass, which fail, and — crucially — how the failures pattern across the wafer and across the design's blocks. Yield AGTs record the result die by die, extending each die's digital thread.

InputProcessed wafers, sort test program
OutputWafer maps, known-good dies
RecordedYield AGT data, fail-pattern maps, per-die thread updates
GateYield and fail patterns within expected envelope — or attribution starts

Feedback: fail patterns are attributed to stages and blocks, and the attribution goes back to design.

Stage 6 — 3D packaging

Stacking changes the physics: thermal dissipation paths, TSV stress, and interconnect behavior all shift once dies are stacked. Packaging-stage agents record what the stack actually does — measured signal integrity and thermal behavior — rather than what the pre-stack models assumed.

InputKnown-good dies, known-good interposers
OutputAssembled 3D packages
RecordedPackaging AGT data, thermal and signal-integrity measurements
GatePackage-level electrical and thermal checks pass

Feedback: stack-level phenomenology returns to both chip and package design.

Stage 7 — Final test (FT)

The packaged part faces the full specification. FT results close the loop on the design's margin claims: a part that passed sort but runs hot, slow, or marginal at final test is exactly the signal the pre-tape-out margin analysis was trying to predict — and any miss here is training data for the next prediction.

InputAssembled packages, final test program
OutputQualified packaged parts, bin data
RecordedFT results per die, joined to the full upstream thread
GateFull-spec pass, with margin distribution recorded — not just pass/fail

Feedback: margin distribution at FT is compared against the Stage 2 prediction; discrepancies recalibrate the model.

Stage 8 — PCBA assembly

The chip meets the board. Assembly introduces its own variables — solder, placement, board-level thermals — and its own failure modes, which are easily mistaken for chip problems. Recording this stage separately keeps attribution honest: board-level issues are identified as board-level.

InputQualified parts, PCB, assembly process
OutputAssembled boards
RecordedAssembly yield, board-level failure records
GateBoard-level test pass

Feedback: assembly-stage failures are separated from silicon failures in the record.

Stage 9 — System test & the control loop

The final stage is also the loop's return path. System-level behavior — the product running as a product — is the last phenomenology recorded, and the system control loop carries everything learned across all nine stages back to Stage 1: updated anticipated process values, sharper margin models, and design recommendations for the next chip.

InputAssembled boards, system test suite
OutputShipping systems — and a complete per-die digital thread
RecordedSystem-level results, full-flow attribution summary
GateSystem qualification

Feedback: the full cycle's findings update the models the next design starts from. The flow is a loop, not a line.

Four phases, one loop

The nine stages are the territory; the four phases are how APFEA works that territory. Design interactively with the process in the loop, record everything during fabrication, analyze the phenomenology, and feed the conclusions back — dynamically, not in a post-mortem report.

PHASE 1

Interactive design

The AI PDK: the designer works against anticipated process behavior, with real-time Vt pre-estimation, rather than against nominal PDK values alone.

  • Anticipated Vt = f(PDK test-chip vs. customer-chip differences, history)
  • Margin visible per block, as you design

PHASE 2

Fabrication & AGT recording

As lots run, agent (AGT) records accumulate at every level — wafer-lot, chamber, 3D packaging, yield — building a unique digital thread for every die.

  • Per-stage recording, fixed in advance
  • One continuous thread per die, design to system

PHASE 3

Phenomenological analysis

An AI analysis engine works on what was actually measured: signal integrity, thermal dissipation, TSV stress zones — the phenomenology of this process on this design.

  • Measured behavior, not model assumptions
  • Failures attributed to stage and block

PHASE 4

Dynamic feedback

Findings become action: Pareto-frontier optimization across the design trade-offs, updated process values, and automated design recommendations.

  • Vtnew = f(AGT data)
  • Recommendations issued while redesign is still cheap

Phase 4's output is Phase 1's input: the loop closes, and each cycle starts from measured reality rather than from the nominal PDK.

The pre-emptive strike: design for the silicon you'll actually get

The PDK gives you Vt0 — the nominal threshold voltage. Production silicon for your chip, with your chip's size and fabric, will sit somewhere else. APFEA's core technical move is to estimate that offset up front and design to the anticipated values from the start:

Design target: Vtanticipated = Vt0 ± x%

The offset x is estimated from PDK test-chip versus customer-chip differences (chip size, fabric, and similar factors) plus historical data. The worked example in the APFEA document:

Device flavorAnticipated Vt (worked example)
Regular-Vt (RVT)RVT0 + 2%
Low-Vt (LVT)LVT0 − 6%
Ultra-low-Vt (ULVT)ULVT0 − 4%

Then prove it, before tape-out

Designing to anticipated values is only half the move. After design and before tape-out, run corner-lot-split DOE simulations — device thresholds swept to ±3σ, exactly as a foundry splits real corner lots — across the functional blocks and then the full chip. Any block whose design margin comes out too small under those conditions gets redesigned. Now, not after silicon.

Why “pre-emptive”

A mask-fix re-tape-out is the most expensive way to learn that a block's margin was too small: new masks, a new fab cycle, a slipped schedule. The same lesson, learned in simulation before tape-out, costs engineering hours. APFEA's whole architecture exists to move that lesson as early as possible — and to make it a routine check, not a heroic save.

The agent: a control tower, not an autopilot

APFEA's software side is the Foundry Process Agent — a working prototype (v0.3) that implements the nine-stage flow above. It runs a continuous agent loop over the recorded flow:

Observe

Read the per-stage records: inputs, outputs, recorded data, gate results.

→

Reason

Attribute deviations: which stage, which block, which assumption broke?

→

Act

Issue findings and recommendations — a HOLD on an at-risk block, a redesign list, an updated Vt model.

→

Learn

Fold measured reality back into the models the next design — and the next lot — starts from.

↺  and around again, for every lot and every design revision.

What it does

  • Walks all nine stages, each with its input / output / recorded data / gate / feedback fixed in advance
  • Contrasts verdicts — e.g. an SRAM block held at its specification limit versus sent back for redesign — from the same margin data
  • Produces agent, pre-tape-out margin, and control-tower reports
  • Runs in both foundry and IDM modes, with an automated test suite
Code & reports on GitHub ↗

What it does not do

The control tower is planning-level. It observes, attributes, and advises. It does not control a fab, it does not change recipes, and it does not make tape-out decisions — those stay with the engineers and the design house. Its demonstration runs on synthetic data: the point is the flow, the gates, and the feedback structure, not any particular fab's numbers.

DemoSoC-1: one pass through the tower

The prototype ships with a built-in demonstration design, DemoSoC-1 (rev A) — a large SoC ASIC with a CPU cluster, GPU/fabric, SRAM cache, 112G SerDes, PLL, and IO bank. Running the agent over its recorded stage entries produces a control-tower report. Headline results from the demo run:

Design DOEPASS — the pre-tape-out corner-lot-split check clears
Fab refitVT REFIT APPLIED — silicon refit cuts the estimated Vt error from 0.50% to 0.26%
3D packaging gateMULTI-DIE — main SoC ASIC plus companion DRAM, HBM, photonics, and analog dies, thermal within plan
Block healthSRAM cache flagged RED → action differs by mode (below)

The SRAM verdict is the demo's clearest lesson in why mode matters. In foundry mode the action is HOLD SPEC: the SRAM is foundry-fixed and stays at Vt0, so the remedy is CMOS-side only, holding the LVT center within ±4.0% of Vt0 — no redesign is demanded of a block the design house cannot change. Run the same data in IDM mode, where the process is in-house, and the verdict becomes REDESIGN. Same silicon data, different owner of the process — different correct action.

The honest boundary: what APFEA is — and is not

A proposal earns trust by labelling itself accurately. APFEA is one engineer's approach, written between jobs and unaffiliated with any company, firm, or government organization — offered openly, with its limits stated up front.

Is APFEA running in a production fab today?

No. What exists is the full proposal document and a working software prototype of the Foundry Process Agent (v0.3), demonstrated on synthetic data. No production deployment is claimed. Status: proposal + prototype

Has it eliminated a real re-tape-out yet?

That is the goal, not yet a result. The pre-emptive method — anticipated-Vt design targets plus pre-tape-out corner-lot-split simulation — is the mechanism proposed to get there; its worked examples come from the proposal document, and field validation with a design-house partner is the open next step. Goal, not yet a measured result

Who may use APFEA, and on what terms?

APFEA is released as a defensive publication: free of charge to all US semiconductor companies, to advance the US semiconductor industry. Does APFEA replace the foundry relationship, or the process guru?

No — it depends on both. The agents amplify the process guru's reach across nine stages; they don't substitute for process judgment, and the design-house / foundry interface still needs the humans who understand each side. The claim is narrower: that interface should run on recorded, shared data instead of assumptions. Design principle
